The platform hop is an EXPANSION operator on this corpus: it can only add
candidates, never remove them. It buys reach into apps AG cannot see, at the
cost of ambiguity. Useful as corroboration for a candidate another signal
already nominated; not usable as a standalone resolver.

========================================================================
rule                                   app keys  collide  AG hits plat hits
N0 raw                                      504        0        3         4
N1 case+whitespace                          504        0        3         4
N2 +punctuation                             504        0        3         4
N3 +strip ' - SO'                           504        0        3         4
N4 +strip env suffix                        504        0        3         4
N5 +strip region suffix                     501        3        6         4
N6 alnum-only (model AG normalizer)         504        0        4         4

Collisions introduced by the aggressive rules (N5), examples:
  'COMMERCIAL COCKPIT'  <-  ['Commercial Cockpit', 'COMMERCIAL COCKPIT EUROPE']
  'MOST VALUABLE STORE'  <-  ['MOST VALUABLE STORE AMESA APAC', 'MOST VALUABLE STORE AMESA APAC - INDIA']
  'SDNA'  <-  ['SDNA GLOBAL', 'SDNA GLOBAL-India']

Reading: N1-N4 and N6 introduce ZERO collisions on the 504-name namespace,
so they are safe - but on the namespaces that ARE loaded they gain almost
nothing (3-4 cross-namespace hits out of 250 AG names). N5, the region-suffix
strip, is the first rule that costs more than it returns: +3 cross-namespace
hits, -3 distinct applications. It must not be adopted.
The Service Offering values that motivated this ladder are NOT in the loaded
corpus, so the coverage side of the table cannot be completed here - only the
collision side. The ' - SO' strip is safe here; its reported 0% -> 6.4% payoff
was measured elsewhere and is not reproducible in this container.

Every one of the top drivers is a generic English or region word - MOBILE,
AMESA, INDIA, ORDER, APPLICATION, MONITORING. Loosening the matcher moved
coverage from 0% to 66.5% of signatures and produced no identifying evidence
whatsoever. This is the false-positive cost the brief asks to weigh, measured
rather than asserted: coverage is trivially purchasable and worth nothing on
its own.

Text matching on this corpus is a ZERO-COVERAGE signal, not the
high-coverage / high-ambiguity signal reported from the ticket sample.
The two are not comparable: recurring signatures are stemmed, deduplicated
machine text, and Short Description is raw per-ticket text that is not
loaded here. Neither result transfers to the other.

Convergence: 0 signatures. Conflict: 0 signatures. Both zero,
and not because the signals agree - because they never both fire. On the
loaded corpus exactly one generator ever produces a candidate, so there is
nothing to combine. EVIDENCE CONVERGENCE and EVIDENCE CONFLICT are therefore
NOT MEASURABLE here. The ensemble question the brief asks - how much
ambiguity is resolved by combining independent evidence - cannot be answered
against this container's data at all, in either direction.

Measurement limit: top_ag is the modal group for a signature, not the group
of any individual incident, so even a non-zero result would have bounded the
shape of convergence rather than measured it per ticket. And convergence is
not correctness: two signals can agree and both be wrong. Without a label
that stays unknowable.
